# Fix the Basics: Infrastructure, Affordability and Reliable Services

## Current published commitment

> Publish a four-year recovery path, evaluate trenchless alternatives, launch the Port Hope Living Lab and report the results through a public infrastructure ledger.

Port Hope has started moving in the right direction. It is still funding only about 37 cents of every dollar required annually for tax-supported infrastructure.

The campaign's position is: keep the progress, accelerate the recovery, and combine disciplined municipal funding, better project coordination, trenchless rehabilitation, grants, industry and research partnerships, responsible financing and controlled real-world pilots to reduce lifecycle costs and accelerate renewal.

## Public purpose or intended benefit

Roads, bridges, culverts, watermains, sewers, stormwater systems, municipal buildings, vehicles and equipment all wear out. The campaign says Port Hope should plan and pay for that work before failures make it more expensive.

The published plan is intended to protect essential services, reduce emergency repairs and repeated disruption, improve flood and stormwater resilience, bring outside funding to the table and make infrastructure choices visible to residents in Ward 1 and Ward 2.

## Existing implementation details

### Establish a four-year infrastructure funding floor

- Protect the recurring infrastructure capacity already created and establish a four-year minimum funding path that residents can inspect.
- Publish three honest budget choices: the effect of no increase, the recommended budget and an enhanced infrastructure option.
- Prevent one-time reserve withdrawals from being presented as permanent funding progress.
- Disclose reserve and debt consequences before approving major projects.
- Use long-term financing only for long-lived assets where the repayment plan is affordable and the full cost is public.
- Publish what would be delayed or reduced under lower-funding alternatives.
- Report consultant costs, expected deliverables and whether the work produced usable lifecycle value.

### Adopt a trenchless-first review

Before excavating a road for underground infrastructure, determine whether a trenchless method can deliver a safer, less disruptive or lower-lifecycle-cost result.

The review will compare lifecycle cost, expected service life, engineering suitability, future capacity, restoration cost, construction disruption, environmental and contaminated-soil risk, timeline, warranty and contractor capability.

### Create the Port Hope Living Lab

Invite companies, universities, utilities, researchers and community partners to propose solutions to clearly defined municipal challenges.

Begin with small, time-limited pilots, publish independent results and move successful ideas through lawful competitive procurement.

Published safeguards include:

- define success and failure before a pilot begins
- set a fixed municipal contribution and maximum financial exposure
- use a fixed pilot term and clear end date
- require engineering, safety, insurance and regulatory review
- complete privacy and cybersecurity review when data or connected systems are involved
- use independent technical validation
- publish complete results and lessons learned
- keep future procurement open and competitive
- preserve portability and municipal control of essential data
- require competitive procurement before any major commercial rollout

The proposed initial local pilot is **FlowWatch Port Hope**, a focused, capped test of low-cost monitoring for selected culverts, drainage routes or flood-prone infrastructure. It will scale only when the evidence supports it.

### Bring outside money to the table

Build a wider funding stack through grants, research funding, partner contributions, vendor-funded pilots, development contributions, host-community agreements, responsible long-term financing and documented lifecycle savings.

Municipal standards, procurement, priorities and public assets remain under public control.

### Dig once and coordinate the work

Create a recurring capital coordination table and a shared multi-year corridor map.

Before rebuilding a road or opening a corridor, determine what other road, water, wastewater, stormwater, utility, telecommunications, remediation or major-development work is expected in the same location.

Major projects will document the coordination opportunities considered. When major repeat excavation is unavoidable, publish why the work could not be coordinated.

### Publish the infrastructure ledger

Publish a plain-language ledger showing the status of every major road, pipe, bridge, culvert and municipal facility, including:

- asset condition, service and safety risk, and project priority
- original and current budget, funding sources, and reserve or debt impact
- expected and actual dates, reasons for delay, and consequences of deferral
- planned versus emergency work, grants and partner contributions
- projected lifecycle savings, pilot results, status and next decision point

Security-sensitive infrastructure, active procurement, personal information and legally privileged material remain protected.

### First 100 days

1. Publish one reconciled infrastructure baseline explaining the approximately $8.6 million tax-supported gap and the roughly $12 million consolidated gap.
2. Present a four-year recurring infrastructure funding path with reserve, debt and tax implications.
3. Identify at least three upcoming underground projects for trenchless feasibility screening.
4. Establish the cross-agency capital coordination table.
5. Publish the first Port Hope Living Lab challenge.
6. Develop the scope, safeguards and evaluation criteria for a focused FlowWatch Port Hope pilot.
7. Publish the first version of the public infrastructure ledger.
8. Report publicly on grants, external contributions and funding applications already available or in progress.

### Public measurement

The annual infrastructure report will track the funded share and remaining gaps, recurring capacity, planned versus emergency repair spending, project delivery, infrastructure renewed, reserve activity, debt, lifecycle savings, external dollars, pilot outcomes, reasons for deferrals, service interruptions and Ward 1 and Ward 2 capital needs with context.

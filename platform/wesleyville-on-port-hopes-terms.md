# Wesleyville on Port Hope's Terms

## Current published commitment

> I support new nuclear development at Wesleyville. I will use Port Hope's support and leverage to negotiate a binding and public Host Municipal Agreement that fully protects taxpayers, funds project-driven municipal needs and includes a fully funded Port Hope Energy Dividend.

Jordan's support for the project is firm. The condition applies to the final Host Municipal Agreement Port Hope signs, not to whether Jordan supports new nuclear development. Jordan will not support the municipality signing a final agreement that shifts project-driven costs onto existing taxpayers or excludes a fully funded direct resident Energy Dividend consistent with the published staged plan.

## Public purpose or intended benefit

Use Port Hope's leverage to pursue a generational opportunity while protecting existing taxpayers, planning infrastructure and emergency readiness, creating local workforce and supplier opportunities, and leaving a permanent community benefit.

## Existing implementation details

- Establish municipal cost, capacity and emergency-readiness baselines before Port Hope signs final agreements or accepts project-driven municipal obligations.
- Negotiate a binding and public Host Municipal Agreement with milestone-based upfront infrastructure contributions and ongoing host-community payments.
- Seek advance funding for reasonably attributable project-driven roads, traffic, water, wastewater, stormwater, fire, emergency planning, recreation, municipal staffing, technical review and administrative capacity.
- Require lifecycle funding, escalation clauses, audit rights, public reporting, dispute mechanisms and corrective-action tracking.
- Require a staged, directly funded Port Hope Energy Dividend as a term of the final Host Municipal Agreement Jordan will support, with the first fully funded phase beginning immediately when the agreement is signed and the required first-year funding, administration costs and 24-month reserve are secured.
- Keep nuclear safety regulation with the Canadian Nuclear Safety Commission while Port Hope owns local emergency readiness.
- Participate fully in regulatory and federal impact-assessment processes and retain independent advisers of the municipality's choosing at project cost.
- Maintain regular emergency exercises, plain-language monitoring and regulatory reporting, a community liaison process and protection for genuinely sensitive security information.
- Create lawful workforce and supplier pathways through occupation forecasts, apprenticeships, pre-apprenticeships, paid co-ops or placements, education and labour partnerships, supplier development and public hiring, training and procurement reporting.
- Plan housing, roads, emergency services and community infrastructure so growth is sequenced with capacity and benefits the whole municipality, including Ward 2.
- Design project-funded upgrades for the community's long-term use, not only the construction period.
- Publish agreements, audits, outstanding requirements, unresolved conditions and results.

The project structure, Ontario Power Generation and the Province should make fair upfront and ongoing contributions toward reasonably attributable project-driven municipal costs. The campaign will not rely on hoped-for future assessment growth as a substitute for negotiated protection.

Filing nomination papers does not make any of us nuclear scientists. The mayor's job is to ask hard questions, demand clear public answers, maintain local emergency readiness and represent Port Hope in the process. The regulator's job is to decide whether the project meets nuclear safety and licensing requirements. Port Hope should not substitute political guesswork for independent technical regulation.

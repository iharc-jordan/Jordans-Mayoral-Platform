# Wesleyville on Port Hope's Terms

## Current published commitment

> Negotiate a binding and public Host Municipal Agreement that protects Port Hope from project-driven costs, strengthens local infrastructure and funds a staged Port Hope Energy Dividend.

Jordan supports new nuclear development at Wesleyville and will seek binding local terms that protect municipal finances, infrastructure, emergency readiness and community life while delivering a direct household benefit.

## Public purpose or intended benefit

Use Port Hope's leverage to secure a generational opportunity while protecting existing taxpayers, planning infrastructure and emergency readiness, creating local workforce and supplier opportunities, and leaving a permanent community benefit.

## Existing implementation details

- Establish municipal cost, capacity and emergency-readiness baselines before major commitments are made.
- Negotiate a binding and public Host Municipal Agreement with milestone-based upfront infrastructure contributions and ongoing host-community payments.
- Seek advance funding for reasonably attributable project-driven roads, traffic, water, wastewater, stormwater, fire, emergency planning, recreation, municipal staffing, technical review and administrative capacity.
- Require lifecycle funding, escalation clauses, audit rights, public reporting, dispute mechanisms and corrective-action tracking.
- Make a staged, directly funded Port Hope Energy Dividend a formal negotiating objective, with the first fully funded phase targeted before the end of the 2026-2030 council term.
- Keep nuclear safety regulation with the Canadian Nuclear Safety Commission while Port Hope owns local emergency readiness.
- Participate fully in regulatory processes and retain independent advisers of the municipality's choosing at project cost.
- Maintain regular emergency exercises, plain-language monitoring and regulatory reporting, a community liaison process and protection for genuinely sensitive security information.
- Create lawful workforce and supplier pathways through occupation forecasts, apprenticeships, pre-apprenticeships, paid co-ops or placements, education and labour partnerships, supplier development and public hiring, training and procurement reporting.
- Plan housing, roads, emergency services and community infrastructure so growth is sequenced with capacity and benefits the whole municipality, including Ward 2.
- Design project-funded upgrades for the community's long-term use, not only the construction period.
- Publish agreements, audits, outstanding requirements, unresolved conditions and results.

The project structure, Ontario Power Generation and the Province should make fair upfront and ongoing contributions toward reasonably attributable project-driven municipal costs. The campaign will not rely on hoped-for future assessment growth as a substitute for negotiated protection.

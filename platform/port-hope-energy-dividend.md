# Port Hope Energy Dividend

## Current published commitment

> I support new nuclear development at Wesleyville. I will not support Port Hope signing a final Host Municipal Agreement unless it fully protects taxpayers and infrastructure, funds local readiness and includes a fully funded resident Energy Dividend consistent with the published staged plan.

Support for the nuclear project is firm. Support for Port Hope's final Host Municipal Agreement depends on the agreement meeting Port Hope's terms. The Energy Dividend is a required component of the final municipal agreement Jordan will support.

The published offer is a staged annual Energy Dividend paid directly to eligible Port Hope households and funded through a binding Host Municipal Agreement. The first stage begins immediately when the agreement is signed and the required first-year funding, administration costs and 24-month reserve are secured.

The campaign's public principle is: **Port Hope hosts it. Port Hope shares in it.**

## Public purpose or intended benefit

Give eligible Port Hope households a direct community benefit tied to a generation-spanning project. The resident benefit is separate from project-cost reimbursement, infrastructure and growth-readiness funding, and other municipal revenue.

## Existing implementation details

### Staged household targets

Each stage replaces the previous stage. The amounts do not stack.

| Project stage | Annual household maximum | Quarterly maximum | Public trigger |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Development | $500 | $125 | Binding Host Municipal Agreement signed, with the first year of rebates, administration and 24-month reserve secured |
| Major construction | $1,000 | $250 | Major construction begins and the larger reserve is secured |
| Electricity generation | $1,500 | $375 | Generation begins and long-term host funding is secured |

The published working formula is:

| Project stage | Eligible adult | Eligible dependent | Household maximum |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Development | $200 a year | $100 a year | $500 a year |
| Major construction | $400 a year | $200 a year | $1,000 a year |
| Electricity generation | $600 a year | $300 a year | $1,500 a year |

The $500, $1,000 and $1,500 figures are annual maximum campaign targets. The per-person formula is a working model that must be supported by the final agreement and independent modelling.

### Delivery and eligibility

The published delivery model includes:

- quarterly payments
- direct deposit as the normal method, with mailed cheque or another no-fee accessible option
- annual verification of principal residence and household composition
- published rules, written reasons and a no-fee appeal
- equal terms for renters and homeowners
- eligibility following the resident within Port Hope rather than attaching to a property
- a published residency baseline
- transition rules for future long-term residents
- a planning model requiring at least one adult in the household to have lived in Port Hope for 24 of the preceding 30 months, measured against a Residency Baseline Date tied to the binding Host Municipal Agreement
- published rules for normal household changes

### Funding and taxpayer protection

The project funds the dividend. It is never backed by Port Hope property taxes.

No stage begins until the first year of rebates, administration and a reserve covering the next 24 months are funded or covered by enforceable security.

The published plan separates three obligations:

1. project-cost reimbursement for planning, legal, engineering, emergency and regulatory costs caused by the project
2. separate upfront and milestone funding for infrastructure and growth readiness
3. a resident Energy Dividend formula funding rebates, reserve, administration, audit and enforcement

Port Hope does not borrow, raise property taxes or drain municipal reserves to keep the program running.

### Safeguards and administration

The published safeguards include:

- milestone-based increases only after the relevant project stage is reached and the larger funding obligation is secured
- maintaining the last fully funded stage during a project delay
- an orderly reserve-funded wind-down if the project is cancelled or funding ends
- obligations that survive a sale, restructuring or transfer of the project
- a legally restricted fund or equivalent vehicle
- annual independent audit and public solvency reporting
- escalation clauses, audit rights, dispute resolution and enforceable sponsor security
- personal information limited to identity, residency, household composition and payment-routing needs

The plan's funding test is: **No binding agreement. No secured money. No rebate.**

### Public cost illustration

Using the published 2021 count of 7,315 households, the campaign plan presents these upper-bound illustrations if every qualifying household received the maximum:

| Annual household maximum | Upper-bound annual rebates | Two-year rebate reserve |
|---|---:|---:|
| $500 | $3.66 million | $7.32 million |
| $1,000 | $7.32 million | $14.63 million |
| $1,500 | $10.97 million | $21.95 million |

Administration, audit, appeals, payment processing and contingency would be funded on top of the rebate reserve.

### Delivery roadmap

1. **First 100 days:** Set the Host Municipal Agreement negotiating mandate, commission independent legal and financial modelling, and publish the resident-benefit objective.
2. **2027:** Develop the formula, eligibility baseline, direct-payment system, independent administration and project-funding security. Publish draft terms before a final vote.
3. **2028-2029:** Establish the restricted fund, complete privacy and tax reviews, test the payment system and secure the first two years of rebates plus administration.
4. **Host Municipal Agreement signing:** Launch the fully funded development stage immediately, paying up to $500 annually through quarterly direct rebates, once the first year of rebates, administration and the required 24-month reserve are secured.
5. **As the project advances:** Increase to the $1,000 and $1,500 stages only when construction and generation milestones are reached and the required money is secured.

### Published context

The Energy Dividend is a required campaign term of the final municipal agreement Jordan will support. The $500, $1,000 and $1,500 figures remain annual maximum campaign targets tied to project stages and secured funding; final amounts and administration remain subject to the binding agreement and independent modelling. It is not a current project-owner offer, approved municipal program or existing resident entitlement. The project is not yet approved, licensed, constructed or guaranteed.

No payment begins until the relevant funding, administration and reserve requirements are met. If funding stops, the program freezes or winds down without a property-tax backstop.

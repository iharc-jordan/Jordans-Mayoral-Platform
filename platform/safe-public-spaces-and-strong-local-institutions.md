# Safe Public Spaces and Strong Local Institutions

## Current published commitment

> Publish service and escalation standards while building a coordinated Town, County, police and health response.

Jordan will keep Port Hope Police local, accountable and properly resourced while respecting the Chief's operational independence and the Police Services Board's civilian governance role.

## Public purpose or intended benefit

Protect the person and protect the public space through safety, dignity, housing, care, shared standards, proportionate enforcement and consistent follow-through.

The campaign says treatment should be easier to enter and harder to fall out of, and residents should be able to see who owns a recurring problem, what happens next and how it is escalated.

## Existing implementation details

- Keep Port Hope Police local and advocate for the resources needed to maintain effective service.
- Respect the operational independence of the Chief and the governance role of the Police Services Board.
- Publish who owns recurring public-space, homelessness and addiction issues, the next action and the escalation route.
- Use municipal maintenance, lighting, washrooms, property standards and environmental design alongside appropriate enforcement.
- Focus enforcement on unsafe conduct, not a person's status.
- Keep public places safe, usable and accessible.
- Connect people to voluntary treatment, withdrawal support, housing, outreach and mobile mental-health and addiction supports.
- Convene municipal, County, police, hospital, public-health, treatment, housing and community partners and keep carrying files after referral.
- Build a clear local pathway connecting outreach, emergency response, withdrawal support, treatment, housing and follow-up.
- Advocate through Northumberland County and directly to the Province, escalating and reporting publicly when identified gaps remain unresolved.
- Support scattered-site housing, rent supplements, portable care, landlord protection and tenancy stabilization.
- Publish hours, capacity and activation decisions for warming responses, protect staff, guests and nearby residents, track use and outcomes, and review whether repeated emergency use is producing a better pathway.
- Use clinical evidence, independent legal safeguards, rights advice, the least restrictive lawful response, regular reassessment and clear follow-up in any rights-protected intervention.
- Never punish or confine someone merely for homelessness, addiction or drug use.

Public measures include response and resolution times, repeat locations, unresolved ownership gaps, housing placements, referral completion, failed handoffs, treatment and mobile-support access, maintenance and accessibility outcomes, and public confidence indicators.

# Growth Without Losing Port Hope

## Current published commitment

> Prioritize housing on serviced land and set clear, time-bound paths for viable heritage reuse.

Port Hope should grow deliberately, at a pace its infrastructure and services can support, with more housing and investment that strengthen its character.

## Public purpose or intended benefit

Protect Port Hope's character while making room for renters, younger families, new residents and people who need affordable or supportive housing.

Development should be judged on servicing, design, safety and fit. Growth should pay its fair share through development charges, project agreements and infrastructure contributions that protect existing taxpayers.

## Existing implementation details

- Focus new housing and investment on serviced or previously developed land wherever practical.
- Create clearer paths for Additional Residential Units, thoughtful infill, duplexes, triplexes and viable heritage reuse where conditions support them.
- Support upper-floor downtown homes, adaptive reuse, smaller and accessible homes, mixed-income development and workforce housing.
- Make ordinary homes and portable support the default where a resident's needs allow it, including rent supplements, ordinary apartments, scattered-site housing, portable case management, landlord support and tenancy stabilization.
- Publish objective rules for height, drainage, trees, parking, fire access, streetscape, infrastructure and neighbouring properties.
- Track approval times and remove avoidable barriers on already serviced land.
- Prevent demolition by neglect and establish clear decision timelines.
- When Council rejects a viable heritage reuse proposal, require it to explain the alternative path, likely funding source and timeline.
- Use a preserve, reuse, adapt, salvage and publicly document sequence for significant heritage.
- When major alteration or demolition of a significant property is approved, require developer-funded, publicly accessible digital preservation. Published examples include laser scanning, photogrammetry, detailed 3D models, browser tours, oral histories, library or archive access, durable formats and funded maintenance.
- Report the amount of agricultural land protected and the share of development occurring on serviced or previously developed land.

Public measures include housing choices created, approval timelines, servicing barriers removed, vacant buildings returned to use, heritage properties stabilized or reused, and public digital-preservation projects delivered and maintained.
